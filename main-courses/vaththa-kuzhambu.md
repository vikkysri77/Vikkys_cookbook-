# Vaththa Kuzhambu

A traditional South Indian tangy and spicy curry made with dried vegetables (vaththa). This aromatic dish is rich in flavors from tamarind, spices, and slow-cooked goodness. Perfect served with steamed rice or roti.

## Prep Time
20 minutes

## Cook Time
20 minutes

## Servings
4-6

## Ingredients

### Base Ingredients
- 3 tbsp oil or ghee
- 2 large onions, finely sliced
- 10 garlic cloves, minced
- 2 tbsp tomato puree
- 1/2 tumbler tamarind water (approximately 1 cup)
- Salt to taste
- 1 tsp jaggery (vellam)

### Dried Vegetables (Vaththa)
- 150g dried vegetables (typically a mix of dried brinjal, dried beans, dried okra, dried drumstick)
- 2 tbsp ghee for frying the vaththa

### Spices
- 1 tsp fenugreek seeds (vendhayam)
- 3-4 red chillies (or to taste)
- 1 tsp cumin seeds (seeragam)
- 1/4 tsp turmeric powder
- 1 whole Kashmiri chilli (or 1/2 tsp Kashmiri chilli powder)
- 1 whole dried red chilli (veetu red chilli)
- 2 tbsp coriander powder
- 1 sprig curry leaves
- 1 tsp mustard seeds (for tempering, optional)

## Instructions

1. **Prepare the Dried Vegetables**
   - Rinse the vaththa (dried vegetables) gently under water
   - Heat 2 tbsp ghee in a separate pan
   - Fry the dried vegetables until they become slightly crispy and aromatic (2-3 minutes)
   - Set aside on a plate

2. **Prepare the Spice Base**
   - In a mortar and pestle or spice grinder, make a coarse paste with:
     - Fenugreek seeds
     - Red chillies
     - Cumin seeds
     - Kashmiri chilli
     - Dried red chilli
   - Alternatively, grind them into a fine powder

3. **Temper the Oil**
   - Heat 3 tbsp oil in a large, heavy-bottomed pot or kadai
   - Add fenugreek seeds and let them crackle
   - Add mustard seeds (if using) and let them pop

4. **Cook the Base**
   - Add sliced onions and curry leaves
   - Sauté until onions are golden brown and translucent (5-7 minutes)
   - Add minced garlic and cook for 1-2 minutes until fragrant

5. **Add Tomato and Spices**
   - Add tomato puree and stir well
   - Add the spice paste (or powder) and turmeric powder
   - Cook for 2-3 minutes, stirring constantly, until the raw smell of spices disappears
   - Add coriander powder and mix well

6. **Build the Curry**
   - Add the tamarind water gradually while stirring
   - Add salt to taste
   - Bring the mixture to a boil

7. **Add the Fried Vaththa**
   - Add the fried dried vegetables to the curry
   - Stir well to combine
   - Reduce heat to medium

8. **Simmer**
   - Boil for 10 minutes on medium heat, stirring occasionally
   - The curry should develop a rich, deep color
   - Cover with a lid and cook for 5 more minutes
   - Remove the cover and cook for another 5 minutes to reduce excess liquid if needed

9. **Final Seasoning**
   - Add jaggery (a little bit) and stir well
   - This balances the tartness of tamarind with a hint of sweetness
   - Taste and adjust salt and spice levels as needed

## Notes

- **What is Vaththa?** - Vaththa refers to dried vegetables, typically including dried brinjal (eggplant), dried beans, dried okra, and dried drumstick. These are available in Indian grocery stores or online
- **Tamarind Water Preparation** - Soak 2 tbsp tamarind paste in warm water for 10 minutes, then strain to get 1 cup of tamarind water
- **Consistency** - The curry should be thick and gravy-like. If too thick, add more tamarind water; if too thin, simmer longer
- **Spice Level** - Adjust the number and type of chillies based on your heat preference
- **Storage** - Keeps well in the refrigerator for 3-4 days in an airtight container. Flavors deepen after a day
- **Serving Suggestions** - Serve hot with:
  - Steamed white rice or brown rice
  - Roti, parotta, or dosa
  - Flatbreads like chapati
- **Vegetable Substitutions** - If vaththa is unavailable, you can use:
  - Fresh vegetables like brinjal, beans, okra (adjust cooking time to 15-20 minutes)
  - A mix of fresh vegetables with some dried versions
- **Regional Specialty** - This is a traditional Tamil Nadu and Andhra Pradesh curry, often made for special occasions and festivals
- **Make it Vegan** - Already vegan-friendly! Just use vegetable oil instead of ghee
- **Cooking Tips**:
  - Don't skip frying the vaththa in ghee - it brings out their unique flavor
  - Stir frequently to prevent burning at the bottom
  - The "cover for 5 minutes then remove" step helps soften the vegetables while retaining flavor
  - For deeper flavor, make this a day ahead and reheat before serving
